Diego J. González-Hernández PhD Thesis

Enabling, Accelerating, and Improving Inference for the Epoch of Reionization using Machine Learning

ENIGMA group, Department of Physics
University of California, Santa Barbara
Advisor: Dr. Joseph F. Hennawi

Introduction

The Epoch of Reionization (EoR) is the period of time in which the universe went from having a fully neutral Inter-Galactic Medium (IGM) to a fully ionized one, and marks the last major pahse transition of the IGM. Understanding and characterizing the EoR is an important challenge, as it relates to other major cosmological problems such as the formation and evolution of the first galaxies, super massive black holes (SMBHs) and quasars (QSOs), and other related topics. Despite this, the EoR remains loosely constrained. The main challenges can be divided into three: (i) modeling the IGM requires extremely expensive hydrodynamical simulations, making parameter space explorations difficult (ii) while data has become increasingly better, it is still relatively scarce, and properly comparing data against model predictions requires proper forward modeling of mock observations, and (iii) traditional parameter inference assumptions lead to miscalibrated estimates, which in turn can lead to incorrect conclusions. The main objective of my work has been to address these issues by adopting machine learning (ML) algorithms. In particular, I have explored using neural network surrogates for fast parameter inference (Chapter 1), adapting and testing Simulation-Based Inference (SBI) methods for calibrated posterior estimates (Chapters 2, 3, and 4), and doing some preliminary work towards creating a foundational model (FM) for the simulations used to model the IGM (Chapter 5).

Chapter 1

Using Neural Emulators and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo to constrain the Epoch of Reionization’s History with the Lyα Forest Power Spectrum

  • arXiv
  • MNRAS

Light from distant quasars (QSOs) passes through intergalactic hydrogen and leaves a pattern of absorption. Towards the end of the Epoch or Reionziation, these redshifted absorption features create the Lyman-α forest, which allows us to probe the IGM at redshifts between 5 and 6. We created a new dataset of low resolution simulations of the IGM that follow a user defined reionization history. The outputs from these simulations were forward modeled to create a set of mean power spectra and corresponding covariance matrices. For inference, we trained emulators (neural network surrogates) of for both, and accelerated inference by taking advantage of automatic differentiation to allow the usage of Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC, a gradient based modification of the standard Metropolis-Hastings algorithm for Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling). The emulators are able to reproduce the mean and covariance matrices with relative errors of less than one percent, allowing for fast and accurate model evaluation and inference.

Chapter 2

Calibrated Simulation-Based Inference for the Lyman-α Forest Autocorrelation Function at the Epoch of Reionization

  • Overleaf

The typical assumption of a Gaussian likelihood for summary statistics of the Lyman-α forest to constrain the Epoch of Reinization leads to overconfident or biased posterior estimates. To address this, we tested several simulation-based inference (SBI) algorithms, which are a family of methods that use neural networks to approximate calculations involving the likelihood. Specifically we tested Neural Likelihood Estimation (NLE), Contrastive Neural Ratio Estimation (NRE-C), Balanced Neural Ratio Estimation (BNRE), and Neural Posterior Estimation (NPE) on a model of the autocorrelation function of the Lyman-α forest designed to constrain the mean free path of ionizing photons. Compared against a Gaussian likelihood as a baseline, most SBI methods are able to achieve both better calibrated and tigher posteriors, though some differences arise between them. These results demonstrate that SBI methods are able to provide calibrated and tight constrains using existing datasets. This work is to be submitted to MNRAS.

Chapter 3

Calibrated Inference of the IGM Neutral Fraction from Quasar Damping Wing Profiles with Balanced Neural Ratio Estimation

  • Overleaf

The precense of high densities of neutral hydrogen in the intergalactic medium (IGM) produces a resonant absorption feature around the rest frame Lyman-α absorption line, known as the Lyman-α damping wing, which can be observed in high redshift quasars (QSOs). Consequently, it is possible to constrain both the neutral fraction and the QSO lifetime from direct observations. However, the typical assumption of a Gaussian likelihood leads to miscalibrated posteriors. A post hoc correction method exists (which involves performing repeated posterior estimates from mock observations which are used to obtain weights that can be applied to broaden the posteriors themselves). To improve this, we train a Balanced Neural Ratio Estimator (BNRE) on mocks of the Lyman-α damping wing profiles. Compared against the corrected posteriors from the Gaussian likelihood, BNRE is able to obtain estimates that are calibrated without requiring any posterior reweighting. Additionally, the BNRE posteriors match or beat the precision from the corrected Gaussian likelihood, while making the likelihood evaluation approximately 39 times faster. These results show that simulation-based inference methods could enable fast and calibrated posteriors to constrain the EoR using observations of the Lyman-α damping wing observed in high redshift QSOs. This work is to be submitted to MNRAS.

Chapter 4

GapSBI: A Benchmark for Simulation-Based Inference under Missing Data

  • Overleaf

Applications of simulation-based inference (SBI) algorithms, which trains neural networks on simulated data to estimate model parameters, are becoming increasingly common in a variety of fields. In some cases, however, real observations are often incomplete. How missing data affects these estimates from SBI algorithms has not been studied systematically yet. To address this problem, we built GapSBI: Gaps in data in Simulation-Based Inference, a benchmark with four inference problems, two missing data mechanisms ways and three missing data fractions. We test five different methods to handle missing data, keeping a common Neural Posterior Estimation backbone fixed accross all the problem variants and simulation budgets. Accross the methods studied, no single method wins everywhere, with simple approaches being often competitive, stable and cheap, learned reconstruction of the missing values being sometimes unstable when the missing data is structured, and a transformer based approach being robust and well calibrated but much more expensive. These results point at the necessity to further understand how missing data affects inference in SBI methods, and shows potential directions for the development of future methods. This work was accepted at the Machine Learning for the Physical Sciences workshop (ML4PS 2026), and is under review at the International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Statistics (AISTATS 2027).
